About Me

Systemic neuroscientist exploring the machinery of thought.

I have recently successfully defended my dissertation at the Graduate School of Systemic Neurosciences (GSN-LMU) in Munich. My PhD research focused on neural computation and cognitive control in the prefrontal cortex underlying flexible, goal-directed behavior. My translational work is now extending from extracellular electrophysiology in primates and rodents to intraoperative recording in awake patients.

I became a neuroscientist out of a philosophical curiosity about our own being. With a background in liberal arts, I have always seen the phenomenal world as a composition, an art, constructed by our brain. I want to understand what makes us feel, what makes us think, and what makes us who we are.

Research

I use in vivo electrophysiology in subjects performing tasks that require complex cognitive control, to investigate how distributed neural populations encode task structures and guide decision-making.

  • Complementary roles for medial prefrontal cortex and mediodorsal thalamus during cognitive set shifting.
  • Functional organization of the primate prefrontal cortex reflects individual mnemonic strategies.

Life beyond research

I love to travel and to create. To travel is to go where I’ve never been before; to create is to work with things I’ve never touched before.

I’ve watched the sun rise from Mount Machapuchare in Nepal, and set over the Dead Sea in Palestine. I’ve felt the breeze of the Aegean Sea in Greece, and the freezing breath of Lake Baikal in Russia. Yet, my dreams still linger by Qinghai Lake, West Lake, and Dianchi Lake in my homeland. There’s so much more of this world to see.

I enjoy bringing imagination into reality. In my spare time, I design and print 3D models, and sometimes build mechanical kits like Gundam or mecha musume. Recently, inspired by the open-source AI movement, I’ve been exploring how to deploy chatbots locally and integrate them into embedded systems such as the ESP32.
